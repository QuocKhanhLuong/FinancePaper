# Losses, explanation reliability, and attribution methods

**Status:** research note only. No model, training code, or experiment result is implemented here.

**Review date:** 2026-10-03. Sources were checked against an official publisher, conference, proceedings, or repository page where possible. An arXiv or workshop source is labelled explicitly when no archival journal/conference DOI was verified.

## Decisions for the temporal pilot

1. **Default risk loss:** unweighted binary cross-entropy (BCE), followed by probability calibration on the dedicated calibration split. Compare weighted BCE and focal loss as pre-specified ablations. Do not use class weighting, resampling, or focal loss as a novelty claim.
2. **Operating threshold:** select it on development data (or a separately reserved threshold part of the calibration data), after the model and probability calibrator are fixed. Threshold selection changes the decision rule; it does not repair a distorted probability model.
3. **XGBoost:** retain the current XGBoost baseline. Treat `scale_pos_weight = N_negative / N_positive` as a standard cost-sensitive ablation, with independent probability calibration, not as the default explanation-reliability model.
4. **Primary neural attribution:** deterministic Integrated Gradients (IG) on the **raw risk logit**, with a frozen training-split baseline and a fixed mask/delta context for each endpoint. Use the same baseline and integration settings before and after restoration. Aggregate signed attributions over encoded dimensions and months back to original financial fields.
5. **Secondary attribution checks:** GradientSHAP can be a sensitivity analysis for baseline-distribution effects. DeepSHAP/DeepLIFT-SHAP should not be the primary method for a custom GRU-D-like cell until the exact implementation passes completeness and MPS tests; recurrent gates and embedding inputs make support more fragile.
6. **Reliability head:** a selector trained on the verification-defined reason-revision event is a plausible narrow adaptation, but learned explanation uncertainty and selective explanation release are already established. The paper must state the gap as the *target event and protocol* (reasons changing after hidden values are verified), not as the generic idea of predicting explanation reliability.

## Class imbalance and probability calibration

| Method | What is already known | Calibration implication | Pilot use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unweighted BCE/log loss | Proper scoring rule for the observed class prior when the model is correctly specified; the ordinary risk-model baseline. | Best starting point for probabilities. Still calibrate on a held-out split. | **Default.** |
| Weighted BCE / cost-sensitive loss | Standard imbalanced-classification practice; class-weighted risk is established in Xu et al. (ICML 2020). | The population minimizer is a reweighted posterior, not generally the population posterior. Caplin et al. derive the binary correction for weighted proper losses. A held-out calibrator is needed for risk probabilities. | **Required ablation**, using training counts only. |
| XGBoost `scale_pos_weight` | Official XGBoost parameter; the documented heuristic is the negative/positive count ratio. | Changes the effective objective/prior. Calibrate scores independently before interpreting them as default probabilities. | **Standard XGBoost ablation**, not novelty. |
| Focal loss | Lin et al. introduced it for dense detection; it is a known loss, not a credit or temporal novelty. | Charoenphakdee et al. show focal loss is classification-calibrated but not strictly proper: its raw minimizer need not equal the true posterior. Calibration or a posterior correction is required. Mukhoti et al. show that empirical calibration can improve in some deep-learning settings, not that it is universal. | **Required ablation** only; report calibration trade-off. |
| Modified/dual focal variants | Variant-specific proposals exist, but the loss family is established and no variant is justified by the current Taiwan protocol alone. | The calibration behavior is variant- and data-dependent; do not infer calibrated probabilities from a focal-like objective. | **Defer** unless a pre-registered reason and exact source support it. |
| Random over/undersampling, balanced batches, SMOTE | Standard imbalance corrections, not novel. | Resampling changes the effective class prior. van den Goorbergh et al. show common corrections can harm calibration without improving ROC-AUC; Piccininni et al. find very poor calibration after random resampling and recommend prior/plugin correction when resampling is used. | **Do not use as the main training default.** A balanced-batch sensitivity check is acceptable only with calibration. |
| Threshold optimization | Standard decision analysis. It can target recall/cost without changing the score ranking or raw probability model. | Choose the threshold on development data only. A threshold cannot fix Brier/log-loss/calibration errors. | **Use for operating-point reporting**, never test-set tuning. |

The class-imbalance papers are especially relevant because this project releases a risk probability and an explanation, rather than only a hard label. A loss that improves minority recall but changes the probability scale can also change top reasons and reason-revision rates through a different fitted function. Each loss therefore needs the same split, masks, seeds, probability calibration procedure, and attribution protocol.

- Xu, Dan, Khim, Ravikumar, *Class-Weighted Classification: Trade-offs and Robust Approaches*, ICML 2020, [official PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/xu20b.html). Class-weighted classification is a standard imbalanced-risk formulation.
- Charoenphakdee, Lee, Sugiyama, *On Focal Loss for Class-Posterior Probability Estimation: A Theoretical Perspective*, CVPR 2021, DOI `10.1109/CVPR46437.2021.00516`, [official CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Charoenphakdee_On_Focal_Loss_for_Class-Posterior_Probability_Estimation_A_Theoretical_Perspective_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf). Focal loss is classification-calibrated but not strictly proper; the paper gives a posterior transformation.
- Mukhoti et al., *Calibrating Deep Neural Networks using Focal Loss*, NeurIPS 2020, [official NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/aeb7b30ef1d024a76f21a1d40e30c302-Paper.pdf). Empirical deep-learning calibration results are setting-dependent and use held-out calibration procedures.
- Caplin, Martin, Marx, *Calibrating for Class Weights by Modeling Machine Learning*, arXiv 2022, [full preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04613). **Preprint; no archival DOI was verified.** Derives why class weighting shifts the score away from the unweighted posterior and gives an analytic correction under its assumptions.
- van den Goorbergh, van Smeden, Timmerman, Van Calster, *The harm of class imbalance corrections for risk prediction models*, JAMIA 2022, DOI `10.1093/jamia/ocac093`, [official full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9382395/). In logistic-risk simulations, over/under-sampling and SMOTE harmed calibration and did not improve ROC-AUC; threshold changes can achieve similar operating points.
- Piccininni et al., *Understanding random resampling techniques for class imbalance correction and their consequences on calibration and discrimination of clinical risk prediction models*, Journal of Biomedical Informatics 155 (2024) 104666, DOI `10.1016/j.jbi.2024.104666`, [official metadata](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/understanding-random-resampling-techniques-for-class-imbalance-co/). **Abstract/metadata page used for this check.** Reports poor calibration from random resampling and evaluates prior/plugin correction.
- XGBoost documentation, [`scale_pos_weight`](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/latest/parameter.html?highlight=multi+softmax). **Official documentation, not a paper.** It defines the parameter and documents the negative/positive count heuristic.

### Recommendation on the default loss

Use unweighted BCE for the first temporal comparison. A weighted-BCE model and a focal model should be trained under identical splits and masks, then independently calibrated and evaluated with AP, recall/F1, Brier score, log loss, calibration curves, and (if used) ECE. Selective release must be based on the calibrated revision-risk output, not on an uncalibrated focal or weighted score. If minority recall is operationally required, prefer a development-selected threshold or an explicitly calibrated cost-sensitive model over silently changing the training prior.

## Attribution choice for a GRU-D-like model

| Method | Strengths | Risks for this model | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integrated Gradients | Deterministic path method; satisfies Sensitivity and Implementation Invariance; complete up to numerical integration error; direct PyTorch implementation and straightforward raw-logit accounting. | Baseline-sensitive; integer category IDs cannot be interpolated meaningfully; fixed contextual inputs can make attribution conditional on the mask state. | **Primary.** Use a declared training baseline and log convergence/completeness error. |
| GradientSHAP / Expected Gradients | Averages noisy gradients along random baseline-to-input paths; useful for a baseline distribution and sensitivity analysis. | Stochastic variance, seed dependence, and assumptions related to feature independence/local linearity; multiple baselines make current/restored comparisons harder to reproduce. | **Secondary robustness check**, not the paper’s main explanation. |
| DeepSHAP / DeepLIFT-SHAP | Can approximate SHAP-style values efficiently from reference samples. | DeepLIFT rules cover supported operations, but custom recurrent gates and embeddings can be fragile; completeness must be checked for the exact model/version. Background choice also changes the target. | **Defer** unless implementation support and completeness are demonstrated on the exact GRU-D cell. |

Primary sources: Sundararajan, Taly, Yan, *Axiomatic Attribution for Deep Networks*, ICML 2017, [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/sundararajan17a/sundararajan17a.pdf); Lundberg & Lee, *A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions*, NeurIPS 2017, [official paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/7062-a-unified-approach-to-interpreting-model-predictions.pdf); Shrikumar, Greenside, Kundaje, *Learning Important Features Through Propagating Activation Differences*, ICML 2017, [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/shrikumar17a.html); Captum [algorithm comparison](https://captum.ai/docs/algorithms_comparison_matrix), [attribution documentation](https://captum.ai/docs/attribution_algorithms), and [API](https://captum.ai/api/index.html); SHAP [DeepExplainer documentation](https://shap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/shap.DeepExplainer.html).

### Proposed reproducible IG protocol

1. Freeze one **training-split mean encoded baseline** (the current split notation calls this the `train64-mean` baseline) before evaluating development, calibration, or test rows. Keep the baseline out of all restored values and never compute it using test data.
2. Run IG on the scalar **raw risk logit**, not the sigmoid probability. For each input, record the baseline logit, input logit, signed per-input attribution, and completeness residual. The expected identity is `sum(phi) + F(baseline) - F(input) ~= 0`, up to numerical integration error.
3. For a masked endpoint, interpolate only the financial value inputs from the baseline to that endpoint. Hold that endpoint’s mask and delta tensors fixed throughout the path. For the restored endpoint, run a second path with the same value baseline but the restored mask/delta fixed throughout that path. Do not interpolate a mask from missing to observed inside the value attribution; report the mask/delta change as a separate missingness diagnostic.
4. Keep the predictor, value scaling, baseline, integration steps, convergence tolerance, and aggregation rule identical before and after restoration. The current and restored explanations are conditional on their respective observed-information states; this is the intended reason-revision comparison.
5. Encode categorical variables as one-hot float inputs, or use layer IG over the embedding output with a declared reference category/zero embedding. Do not interpolate integer category IDs. For either representation, sum the category dimensions back to the original field.
6. Aggregate attributions by original field and month: sum signed encoded dimensions for `PAY_*`, `BILL_AMT*`, and `PAY_AMT*`; then sum monthly contributions only when reporting a field-level total. Preserve signs and retain the month-level values for revision analysis.
7. Treat mask and delta attributions as diagnostics, not as customer financial reasons, unless a separate pre-specified analysis explicitly reports them. A missingness fraction and per-month mask summary must be logged separately.
8. Compare current/restored top-k fields using the same top-k rule, ties, sign convention, and observed-only eligibility. Never allow a restored field to appear in the current observed-reason list merely because its true value is available offline.
9. Run a small baseline-sensitivity check using fixed train-only background baselines. Do not choose the baseline or integration steps after inspecting test revision rates.

The absolute magnitude of IG, GradientSHAP, and XGBoost SHAP values is not directly comparable across methods. Comparisons should use the method-specific top-k revision metrics and explicitly state the output scale and baseline.

## Reliability-head protocol and leakage controls

The event label must be generated offline as follows: mask a record, obtain the current prediction and attribution from the frozen predictor, restore the hidden true values, obtain the restored prediction and attribution from the same predictor, and compute the pre-specified event `E` from top-k overlap/rank/sign criteria. At inference the head may see the current representation, mask/delta summaries, prediction diagnostics, and current attribution statistics, but never the hidden values, restored attribution, or event label.

Use the protocol’s independent outer partitions (train, development, probability-calibration, reason-risk-calibration, test). A defensible pilot is:

- choose predictor architecture, loss, attribution baseline, and event definition using train/development only;
- fit the predictor using the outer training data and fit raw probability calibration only on the dedicated probability-calibration partition;
- generate selector features and `E` labels using inner cross-fitting within the outer training records. Each inner held-out fold must be scored by a predictor clone that did not train on that fold. This prevents in-sample attributions/representations from making the event artificially easy;
- fit the selector head on those OOF features, using an inner development portion for its hyperparameters. If the predictor is refit after OOF generation, document the representation shift or regenerate OOF features under the final predictor contract;
- use the dedicated reason-risk-calibration partition for selector probability calibration and release-threshold selection. If both calibration and a threshold are estimated there, use cross-fitting or reserve a threshold subpartition; do not report the same rows as an untouched calibration test;
- evaluate risk-coverage, AUROC/AP for predicting `E`, coverage at revision risk at most 10%, and all baselines (missing fraction, prediction variance, SHAP/IG variance, and Monte-Carlo revision score) on the untouched test partition only.

Repeated masked copies of one original customer must remain in that customer’s outer split. The mask generator must be seeded and independent of the target for MCAR. MAR/MNAR masks need their own scenario specification; selecting a mask mechanism after seeing test revision labels is leakage. Changing the attribution baseline, top-k, sign rule, or event threshold after looking at risk-calibration/test outcomes also invalidates the selector result.

The same leakage rule applies to imbalance losses: class weights use training labels only; balanced sampling is performed inside training folds; thresholds use development/calibration data; probability and revision-risk calibration are independent; test probabilities, attributions, and `E` labels are opened once for final evaluation.

## Falsifiers and stopping conditions

The temporal extension should be reported as a negative result if any of the following holds after matched evaluation:

- mask-aware GRU-D does not improve robustness over vanilla GRU and static XGBoost at MCAR 10% and 30% (and the planned MAR scenario), or its gains disappear after probability calibration;
- temporal and static ablations show no repeatable value from the six monthly history steps;
- focal/weighted BCE improves recall but gives materially worse Brier/log loss or calibration and no acceptable operating-point advantage;
- IG reason-revision rate, top-k overlap, or sign/rank stability is no better than the XGBoost reference;
- the learned selector does not beat simple missingness/prediction-variance/attribution-variance baselines on held-out `E` AUROC/AP and risk-coverage, or its calibration fails on the independent reason-risk-calibration/test partitions.

These outcomes would support keeping the paper primarily about verification-aware explanation reliability and retaining LR/XGBoost as the main baselines, rather than making the recurrent architecture the central contribution.

## Bottom-line recommendation for the parent study

Use unweighted BCE plus independent calibration as the first neural baseline; compare weighted BCE and focal loss without treating either as novel. Use deterministic IG on raw logits as the primary recurrent attribution with the fixed training baseline and endpoint-specific fixed mask/delta context. Keep the explanation-reliability claim narrow: a learned selector for the *verification-induced reason-revision event* may be worth testing, but generic explanation uncertainty, selective explanation, imputation uncertainty, and credit explanation stability are already documented. Stage F should remain optional until matched experiments show that the selector adds value beyond simple missingness and attribution-variance baselines.
